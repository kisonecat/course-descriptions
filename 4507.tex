\begin{Purpose}
Starting from questions accessible to Euclid, this course treats
Euclidean, spherical, and hyperbolic geometry from a unified point of
view.

To encourage the student to become a ``do-er'' of mathematics, in this
course essentially write their own ``textbook,'' as the proofs of a
majority of the theorems are left to the student.
\end{Purpose}

\begin{Text}
Open-source textbook:

A workbook of two-dimensional geometries, Clemens and Snapp.

\textbf{Source:} \url{https://github.com/mooculus/advancedGeometry}
\end{Text}

\begin{SuggestedSchedule}
\week{1}{Axioms, incidence geometry, and what a proof requires.}
\week{2}{Neutral geometry: betweenness, congruence, triangles.}
\week{3}{Neutral geometry: exterior angle theorem, Saccheri-Legendre.}
\week{4}{Neutral geometry: quadrilaterals and the parallel postulates.}
\week{5}{Euclidean geometry: parallels, angle sums, similarity.}
\week{6}{Euclidean geometry: area, circles, and classical theorems.}
\week{7}{Euclidean isometries; the group of symmetries.}
\week{8}{Spherical geometry: great circles, distance, lunes.}
\week{9}{Spherical geometry: triangles, area, and the spherical excess.}
\week{10}{Spherical trigonometry; isometries of the sphere.}
\week{11}{Hyperbolic geometry: models, distance, and parallels.}
\week{12}{Hyperbolic geometry: triangles, defect, and area.}
\week{13}{Hyperbolic isometries; Möbius transformations.}
\week{14}{Comparison of the three geometries; connections to high school mathematics.}
\finalsweek{Final examination.}
\end{SuggestedSchedule}
